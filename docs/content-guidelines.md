## 1. Editorial Philosophy: The Four Pillars of Content

To preserve theological reverence, cultural integrity, and academic honesty, all text published on the website must be classified under one of four distinct categories:

```text
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 1. FACT (तथ्य)                                               │
│ Verifiable textual citations from recognized primary canons │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. TRADITION (परंपरा)                                        │
│ Documented cultural narratives, epics, and Puranas          │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. INTERPRETATION (भाष्य / व्याख्या)                         │
│ Modern synthesis connecting ancient concepts to life today   │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 4. CREATIVE DEVOTIONAL COPY (भावानुवाद / मूल लेखन)          │
│ Original website prose written with reverent artistic voice │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 2. Non-Negotiable Editorial Rules

### Rule 1: Zero Fabrication of Scripture
- **Never fabricate a Sanskrit verse.**
- **Never alter a Vedic mantra.**
- **Never claim an original modern English reflection is a quote from an ancient rishi.**
- If a verse is quoted, its exact text, mandala/sukta or chapter/verse number must be logged in `content/sources.md`.

### Rule 2: Clear Textual Stratification
- Clearly label whether an account is:
  - **Vedic** (e.g., Rigveda 10.81, 10.82)
  - **Puranic / Itihasa** (e.g., Vishnu Purana, Mahabharata, Ramayana)
  - **Traditional Craft Lore** (e.g., *Vishvakarma Prakasha*, guild lineages)
  - **Regional Custom** (e.g., Bengal Kanya Sankranti, Gujarat post-Diwali puja)
- Popular internet retellings must never be presented as primary Vedic verses.

### Rule 3: Handling Disagreements or Ambiguity
- When scriptures provide differing accounts (for example, the parentage of Vishwakarma as the son of Prabhasa and Yoga-siddha vs. manifestation from Brahma's mind), note the divergence transparently in `content/sources.md`.
- If an important factual claim cannot be confirmed with a primary text, mark it as **NEEDS VERIFICATION** instead of asserting it as canonical fact.

## 3. Multilingual Integration Principles

The website is **not** a direct tri-lingual clone (we do not translate every sentence word-for-word into three languages). Instead, languages are deployed functionally:

### 1. English: Architectural Clarity & Exploration
- Used as the primary structural, navigation, and explanatory language.
- Tone: Dignified, literate, calm, evocative, editorial.
- Avoid sensationalist or cliché phrases ("mind-blowing", "shocking truth").

### 2. Hindi: Emotional Devotion & Heartfelt Storytelling
- Used for subheadings, narrative reflections, devotional wishes, and katha summaries.
- Tone: Warm, respectful, poetic, culturally rich (*शुद्ध एवं भावपूर्ण हिन्दी*).
- Example: *विश्वकर्मा पूजा की हार्दिक शुभकामनाएँ — कर्म ही पूजा है।*

### 3. Sanskrit: Sacred Timelessness & Mantras
- Used for primordial invocations, Vedic suktas, and foundational mantras.
- Accompanied by IAST transliteration for global pronunciation clarity and thoughtful English translations.
- Example: *ॐ विश्वकर्मणे नमः*

## 5. Visual Captioning & Image Attribution Rules

- Every image must carry an authentic, descriptive `alt` attribute that conveys both visual content and spiritual context.
- When historical artifacts or temple carvings are depicted (e.g., Ellora Kailasa temple, Konark Sun Temple wheels), explicitly state the temple name, location, and century in the caption.
- Never use copyrighted or watermarked imagery. Use public domain, open cultural licenses, or custom-generated artwork.
