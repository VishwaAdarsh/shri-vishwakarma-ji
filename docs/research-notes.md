## 1. Vedic Layer: The Primordial Hymns (Source Type A)

### Rigveda Mandala 10, Suktas 81 & 82 (विश्वकर्मा सूक्तम्)
- **Rishi**: Vishvakarman Bhauvana (विश्वकर्मा भौवनः)
- **Devata**: Vishvakarman (विश्वकर्मा)
- **Chhandas**: Trishtubh (त्रिष्टुप्)
- **Primary Scholarly Repositories**:
  - *Vedic Heritage Portal*, Indira Gandhi National Centre for the Arts (IGNCA), Ministry of Culture, Government of India.
  - *Rigveda Samhita* with Sayana's Bhashya (Vedic Samshodhana Mandala, Pune).
  - Ralph T.H. Griffith, *The Hymns of the Rigveda* (1889).
  - H.H. Wilson, *Rig-Veda Sanhita* (1888).

#### Key Verses and Analysis:
1. **Rigveda 10.81.1**:
   ```text
   य इ॒मा विश्वा॒ भुव॑नानि॒ जुह्व॒दृषि॒र्होता॒ न्यसी॑दत्पि॒ता नः॑ ।
   स आ॒शिषा॒ द्रवि॑णमि॒च्छमा॑नः प्रथम॒च्छदव॑रा॒ँ आ वि॑वेश ॥
   ```
   *Analysis*: Vishvakarman is depicted as the primordial Hotar (sacrificer) who sacrifices all created realms into the cosmic fire, entering subsequent creations to nourish them.

2. **Rigveda 10.81.2**:
   ```text
   किं स्विदासीदधिष्ठानमारम्भणं कतमत्स्वित्कथासीत् ।
   यतो भूमिं जनयन्विश्वकर्मा वि द्यामौर्णोन्महिना विश्वचक्षाः ॥
   ```
   *Analysis*: The rishi asks profound philosophical questions: "What was his station? What was the starting point or support, from which the all-seeing Vishvakarman brought forth the earth and unfolded the heavens in his majesty?"

3. **Rigveda 10.81.3**:
   ```text
   विश्वतश्चक्षुरुत विश्वतोमुखो विश्वतोबाहुरुत विश्वतस्पात् ।
   सं बाहुभ्यां धमति सं पतत्रैर्द्यावाभूमी जनयन्देव एकः ॥
   ```
   *Analysis*: This iconic verse portrays the creator having eyes, mouths, arms, and feet in all directions. He "welds" (*dhamati*) heaven and earth together as a blacksmith blows the forge bellows.

4. **Rigveda 10.82.3**:
   ```text
   यो न॑: पि॒ता ज॑नि॒ता यो वि॑धा॒ता धामा॑नि॒ वेद॒ भुव॑नानि॒ विश्वा॑ ।
   यो दे॒वानां॑ नाम॒धा एक॑ ए॒व तं स॑म्प्र॒श्नं भुव॑ना यन्त्य॒न्या ॥
   ```
   *Analysis*: Identifies Vishvakarman as the sole parent, originator, and arranger of destinations, who alone named the devas.

## 2. Historical & Traditional Textual Layer (Source Type B)

### The 1900 Publication: *श्री विश्वकर्मदेव की कथा : पूजन-विधि सहित*
- **Title**: *श्री विश्वकर्मदेव की कथा : पूजन-विधि सहित* (Shri Vishvakarmadev Ki Katha: Pujan-Vidhi Sahit)
- **Publication Year**: 1900 CE (Vikram Samvat 1957)
- **Historical Context**: Printed during the late 19th and early 20th century publishing movement in North India (associated with presses like Naval Kishore Press, Lucknow, and Khemraj Shrikrishnadass / Venkateshwar Press, Bombay).
- **Structure**:
  1. *Purva-Peethika*: Significance of worshipping Vishwakarma as the protector of human industry.
  2. *Vrat Katha*: Traditional narrative recounting the rewards of righteous craft, honest labor, and regular worship of tools.
  3. *Pujan Vidhi*: Prescribed Sanskrit mantras, dhyana, sixteen upacharas, and congregational aarti tailored for artisans, mechanics, and smiths.
- **Significance for Website**: Establishes that the modern community festival of Vishwakarma Puja has a documented literary lineage exceeding a century of continuous vernacular practice.

### Architectural Canon: *Vishvakarma Prakasha* (विश्वकर्मप्रकाशः)
- **Genre**: Vastu Shastra (Classical Indian Architecture)
- **Key Chapters**:
  - *Bhumi Pariksha* (Soil and site selection).
  - *Dvara Mana* (Proportions of doorways and ventilation).
  - *Griha Vinyasa* (Spatial layout of homes, halls, and water storage).
- **Philosophical Principle**: Every building is a living organism (*Vastu Purusha*) whose energy aligns with the cardinal directions and celestial forces.

## 3. Puranic & Epics Layer (Source Type C)

### Vishnu Purana (Book 3, Chapter 2)
- Recounts the celestial marriage of Vishwakarma's daughter Sanjna to Surya (the Sun).
- To relieve Sanjna from Surya's unbearable radiance, Vishwakarma placed Surya upon his lathe (*bhrami*) and pared down one-eighth of the solar luster.
- From the gathered effulgence, Vishwakarma crafted:
  - The **Sudarshana Chakra** of Bhagwan Vishnu.
  - The **Trishula** of Bhagwan Shiva.
  - The **Shakti** (lance) of Karttikeya / Skanda.
  - The weapons and aerial vehicles of the Devas.

### Mahabharata (Adi Parva, Chapter 66)
- Identifies Vishwakarma as the son of Prabhasa (the eighth Vasu) and Yoga-siddha (sister of Brihaspati).
- Described as:
  > *"The father of all arts, executor of a thousand crafts, carpenter of the gods, fashioner of all celestial ornaments, upon whose craft all human artisans sustain their livelihoods."*

### Valmiki Ramayana (Yuddha Kanda, Sarga 22)
- Recounts the construction of the ocean bridge (*Ram Setu*) across the sea to Lanka.
- The Vanara hero Nala announces that he inherited his engineering genius directly from his father Vishwakarma:
  > *"I am the son of Vishwakarma, born with skill equal to my father's. By my design, a bridge shall be built over the ocean."*

## 4. Regional & Living Tradition Layer (Source Type D)

### Kanya Sankranti Observance
- Unlike lunar Hindu festivals (like Diwali or Holi), Vishwakarma Puja in West Bengal, Bihar, Jharkhand, Odisha, and Assam follows the **Sauramana** (Solar calendar).
- It occurs predictably on **September 16, 17, or 18**, on the day Surya transitions into Kanya (Virgo).
- Traditional practice demands the total cessation of machinery operation on the festival day.

### Five Traditional Artisan Communities (Panchal / Vishwakarma Lineage)
Traditional Indian society organized the Vishwakarma craft fraternity into five principal lineages attributed to the five sons of Vishwakarma:
1. **Manu**: Blacksmiths (*Lohar* / Iron crafts).
2. **Maya**: Woodworkers (*Badhai* / Carpenters).
3. **Tvashta**: Metalsmiths (*Kansakar* / Brass, bronze, copper).
4. **Shilpi**: Stone sculptors (*Murti-kar* / Masons).
5. **Visvajna**: Goldsmiths & jewelers (*Sonar* / Fine metals).
