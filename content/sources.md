# 📚 Source Registry & Textual Framework
## 🕉️ Vishwakarma — The Divine Architect

Every narrative claim, verse, and cultural practice documented on this website is tracked and categorized according to scholarly textual stratification.

---

### Source Type Classification System
- **Source Type A — Vedic**: Primary Samhitas, Brahmanas, and Upanishads (Rigveda, Yajurveda, Atharvaveda).
- **Source Type B — Traditional Textual**: Historical printed literature, Vastu Shastras (*Vishvakarma Prakasha*), Shilpa Shastras, and traditional katha manuals.
- **Source Type C — Puranic & Itihasa**: Major Mahapuranas (Vishnu, Bhagavata, Skanda) and epics (Mahabharata, Ramayana).
- **Source Type D — Regional / Popular Tradition**: Living cultural practices, festival calendars (Kanya Sankranti), and oral craft traditions.

## Source Entries

### Entry 01: The Cosmic All-Seeing Creator
* **Claim**: Vishvakarman is the primordial creator who possesses eyes, mouths, arms, and feet on all sides, welding heaven and earth together.
* **Source Type**: Vedic (Source Type A)
* **Primary Source**: Rigveda Mandala 10, Sukta 81, Verse 3 (१०.८१.३).
* **Translation**: Ralph T.H. Griffith / H.H. Wilson; Vedic Heritage Portal (Indira Gandhi National Centre for the Arts):  
  *"Having eyes on all sides, a face on all sides, arms on all sides, and feet on all sides, the Sole Divine Being, producing heaven and earth, welds them together with his arms and wings."*
* **Interpretation**: Represents the unmanifest cosmic intelligence before sectarian deities developed in later eras; Vishvakarman as the cosmic artisan.
* **Website Treatment**: Hero chapter anchor; presented with full Sanskrit Devanagari text, transliteration, and solemn English translation.
* **Confidence**: High (Direct Samhita verse verified).
* **Notes**: No disagreement. Confirmed in Shukla Yajurveda 17.19 and Taittiriya Samhita 4.6.2.

### Entry 02: The Universal Father and Name-Giver of Gods
* **Claim**: Vishvakarman is our father, creator, arranger of all realms, and the one who bestowed names upon the gods.
* **Source Type**: Vedic (Source Type A)
* **Primary Source**: Rigveda Mandala 10, Sukta 82, Verse 3 (१०.८२.३).
* **Translation**: *यो नः पिता जनिता यो विधाता धामानि वेद भुवनानि विश्वा । यो देवानां नामधा एक एव तं सम्प्रश्नं भुवना यन्त्यन्या ॥*
* **Interpretation**: Theological declaration of single supreme creative principle upon whom all created beings rely for inquiry.
* **Website Treatment**: Quoted in Chapter 01 to emphasize that Vishwakarma's foundation is ancient cosmic philosophy, not mere folklore.
* **Confidence**: High (Direct Samhita verse verified).
* **Notes**: Found also in Atharvaveda parallels.

### Entry 03: The Lathe of the Sun & Creation of Divine Weapons
* **Claim**: Vishwakarma placed Surya on his lathe to shave off a fraction of blinding rays, fashioning Sudarshana Chakra, Shiva's Trishula, and celestial weapons.
* **Source Type**: Puranic (Source Type C)
* **Primary Source**: Vishnu Purana (Book 3, Chapter 2); Markandeya Purana (Sanjna-Surya episode).
* **Translation**: H.H. Wilson, *The Vishnu Purána: A System of Hindu Mythology and Tradition*, 1840:  
  *Vishwakarma placed the Sun on the lathe (*bhrami*) and ground down the eighth part of his effulgence, from which he manufactured the disc of Vishnu, the trident of Hara, the palanquin of Kubera, and the javelin of Karttikeya.*
* **Interpretation**: Allegorical harmonization of extreme cosmic power into structured, usable celestial instruments to maintain cosmic balance.
* **Website Treatment**: Featured in Chapter 03 ("The Divine Creations") as an illustrated narrative card.
* **Confidence**: High (Major Mahapurana source verified).
* **Notes**: Retold with minor variations across Matsya Purana and Harivamsa.

### Entry 04: Master of All Crafts and Architecture of the Devas
* **Claim**: Vishwakarma is the author of all arts, ornaments, and palaces of the devas; maker of Indra's city and the assembly halls.
* **Source Type**: Puranic & Itihasa (Source Type C)
* **Primary Source**: Mahabharata, Adi Parva (Section 66); Bhagavata Purana (Skandha 6).
* **Translation**: P.C. Roy / Kisari Mohan Ganguli, *Mahabharata Adi Parva*:  
  *Vishwakarma was the lord of arts, the creator of a thousand crafts, the carpenter of the gods, the most eminent of artisans, who fashioned all celestial ornaments.*
* **Interpretation**: Solidifies Vishwakarma's role as the divine patron of human technology, mechanics, and design.
* **Website Treatment**: Chapter 02 core foundation.
* **Confidence**: High (Verified in Mahabharata critical edition).
* **Notes**: Establishes divine patron status for mortal artisans.

### Entry 05: Construction of the Ocean Bridge by Nala
* **Claim**: Nala, the Vanara commander and engineer who constructed the Setu to Lanka, was the direct son and inheritor of Vishwakarma's architectural genius.
* **Source Type**: Itihasa (Source Type C)
* **Primary Source**: Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda (Sarga 22, Verses 45–50).
* **Translation**: *मम मातुर्वरो दत्तो मन्दरे विश्वकर्मणा । मया त्वं सदृशः पुत्रो भविष्यति शुभानने ॥* (Valmiki Ramayana 6.22.46):  
  *Vishwakarma granted a boon to my mother on Mount Mandara: "O auspicious lady, a son equal to me in skill will be born to you."*
* **Interpretation**: Demonstrates that architectural prowess is transmitted through lineage and divine blessing, capable of conquering geographical barriers.
* **Website Treatment**: Highlighted under "Divine Architectural Feats" in Chapter 03.
* **Confidence**: High (Direct Valmiki Ramayana verse verified).
* **Notes**: Universally acknowledged across Ramayana traditions (Valmiki, Kamban, Adhyatma).

### Entry 06: Historical Katha and Puja Manual (1900 Publication)
* **Claim**: A documented traditional ritual manual and narrative text exists titled *श्री विश्वकर्मदेव की कथा : पूजन-विधि सहित* published in 1900.
* **Source Type**: Traditional Textual (Source Type B)
* **Primary Source**: *श्री विश्वकर्मदेव की कथा : पूजन-विधि सहित* (Shri Vishvakarmadev Ki Katha: Pujan-Vidhi Sahit), published in Vikram Samvat 1957 / 1900 CE (Naval Kishore Press / Khemraj Shrikrishnadass traditions).
* **Translation**: Hindi ritual commentary and Vrat Katha recitation text used for Vishwakarma Jayanti / Puja ceremonies.
* **Interpretation**: Shows the formalization of congregational and workshop worship of Vishwakarma at the turn of the 20th century in northern and central India.
* **Website Treatment**: Referenced in Chapter 05 and the documentation framework as historical evidence of enduring popular devotion.
* **Confidence**: High (Historical publication record verified).
* **Notes**: Bridges Sanskrit liturgical prescriptions with accessible vernacular Hindi practices.

### Entry 07: Vastu Shastra Treatise — *Vishvakarma Prakasha*
* **Claim**: Ancient architectural manual *Vishvakarma Prakasha* attributes town planning, building orientation, and proportions to Vishwakarma.
* **Source Type**: Traditional Textual (Source Type B)
* **Primary Source**: *विश्वकर्मप्रकाशः* (Vishvakarma Prakasha), foundational treatise on Vastu Shastra.
* **Translation**: Various editions (Venkateshwar Steam Press, Chowkhamba Sanskrit Series).
* **Interpretation**: Classical Indian codification of civil engineering, environmental harmony, soil testing, and temple geometry.
* **Website Treatment**: Cited in Chapter 04 ("The Tradition of Craft") to show the intellectual depth of the Indian architectural tradition.
* **Confidence**: High (Recognized Vastu treatise).
* **Notes**: Attributed to the divine sage Vishwakarma; reflects centuries of practical architectural wisdom.

### Entry 08: Kanya Sankranti & Factory / Tool Worship
* **Claim**: Vishwakarma Puja is celebrated annually on the day Surya enters the Kanya (Virgo) solar sign (usually September 16–18), accompanied by the cessation of factory work and consecration of tools.
* **Source Type**: Regional / Popular Tradition (Source Type D)
* **Primary Source**: Pan-Indian Solar Calendar observances; Panchang records across Bengal, Bihar, Jharkhand, Odisha, Assam, Uttar Pradesh, and Karnataka.
* **Translation**: Customary festival practice.
* **Interpretation**: Unlike lunar festivals that drift across the Gregorian calendar, Vishwakarma Puja in eastern and northern India is tied to solar Kanya Sankranti, symbolizing cosmic alignment and precision.
* **Website Treatment**: Explained clearly in Chapter 05 with explicit labeling as a solar calendar observance.
* **Confidence**: High (Living cultural tradition).
* **Notes**: Note that in parts of Gujarat, Rajasthan, and western India, Vishwakarma Jayanti is also observed on the day after Diwali (associated with Govardhan Puja / New Year) or on Magha Shukla Trayodashi. The website will transparently note both traditions.

### Entry 09: The Mysterious Artisan of Puri Jagannath
* **Claim**: Vishwakarma appeared in the guise of an old artisan (*Ananta Maharana*) to carve the sacred idols of Jagannath, Balabhadra, and Subhadra from the sacred Daru log.
* **Source Type**: Puranic & Temple Tradition (Source Type C & D)
* **Primary Source**: Skanda Purana (Utkala Khanda); Madala Panji (Temple Chronicle of Jagannath Temple, Puri).
* **Translation**: *Traditional accounts of King Indradyumna and Queen Gundicha.*
* **Interpretation**: The unfinished divine forms symbolize that the Supreme Reality cannot be fully delineated by finite human hands; divine art transcends material perfection.
* **Website Treatment**: Included in Chapter 03 / Creations as a sacred legend of humility in craftsmanship.
* **Confidence**: Medium-High (Central to Puri temple tradition; Puranic variations exist).
* **Notes**: Labeled clearly as "Temple Tradition & Puranic Account".
